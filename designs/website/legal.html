4.Usage numbers

Counts and categories about how Artifice is used and how well it works. On by default, easy to turn off, never your content.

The app and command line send usage numbers to PostHog, in the European Union. They include:

  • which features and pages are used, when, and how long pages take to appear;
  • how well each briefing worked: how many parts it had, whether your AI then searched again or opened files itself, whether the task went well, and your thumbs up or down if you gave one;
  • how Artifice is working: how long things take, which AI tools are connected and their versions, and when something fails or crashes (the kind of problem and where in Artifice's code, never the message);
  • counts and token figures in ranges, and the mix of file types in a project (for example, 60% code);
  • your plan and changes to it, such as a trial starting, a plan starting or ending, or a code being redeemed, shown by the code's label, never the code itself or your school's name;
  • the AI model you chose; Artifice's version; your operating system and its version, processor, memory and number of cores;
  • a random ID for your installation.

Linked to your account. When you sign in, these numbers are linked to your account (its ID, email address, how you signed in and your plan), including those sent from that computer before. When you sign out, the computer starts again with a new random ID. When you delete your account, the numbers linked to it are deleted too.

Turning them off. Settings › Privacy › Share usage numbers, or run artifice privacy off. Setting DO_NOT_TRACK=1 or ARTIFICE_TELEMETRY=0 also turns them off. Organisations can turn them off on all their computers (how). Settings › Privacy also lists every kind of event Artifice can send.

5.Examples (off unless you turn them on)

If you choose, real briefings are shared with secrets and personal details removed, so we can see where Artifice gets it wrong.

With Settings › Privacy › Help improve Artifice with examples on, for each briefing Artifice sends:

  • your AI's question, with secrets (keys, tokens, passwords), email addresses, phone, card and ID numbers, IP addresses and your own folder name removed;
  • which files and parts the briefing held: their paths, titles and line ranges, and how much of each;
  • what your AI searched for or opened afterwards, and how the task went.

It never sends what's in your files or the text of the briefing. Before turning it on you can see your latest briefing exactly as it would be sent, and you can leave any project out. Removal of secrets is automatic and careful, but not perfect: don't turn examples on for projects whose file names or questions you can't share. Examples are kept for 90 days. Turning examples off stops them at once; to have ones already sent deleted, write to support@tryartifice.com.

6.Plans and payments

If you pay for Pro, Stripe takes the payment and we never see your card number. We keep your plan, its dates and the computers you use.

Stripe tells us the result: your customer and subscription IDs, your plan and billing period, whether each payment went through, and your invoices. Stripe also works out tax and sends receipts. Stripe uses payment data under its own privacy policy as well, for example to prevent fraud and meet its legal duties.

Your plan. To give you your plan on each of your computers, we keep which plan you have, when it started and ends, and its status. For each computer you sign in on, we keep a random ID, its name, and when it was first and last seen. Artifice keeps a signed copy of your plan on your computer, so it keeps working offline.

Student checks. We send a code to the school email address you give us. Once the check is done, we delete the address and keep only its domain and the date, so we know when to check again.

Access codes. We keep which code you redeemed and when. In usage numbers, a code appears only by its label, such as the event it came from.
